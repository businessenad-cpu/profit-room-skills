# Voice and Writing Rules

Apply these to every chapter, every sentence, regardless of whose voice sample you're matching.

## Reading Level and Sentence Style

- 6th-8th grade reading level. Plain words over jargon. If a technical term is necessary, define it
  in one plain-English clause the first time it appears.
- Short sentences. One idea per sentence. Break up anything running past ~20 words.
- No em dashes. Use a period or comma instead.
- Contractions are fine and often better ("you'll", "here's", "don't") — this is a guide a real
  person is talking through, not a legal document.

## What to Avoid

- Filler openers: "In today's world...", "As we all know...", "Let's dive in..."
- Hedging language: "might", "could", "perhaps", "in some cases" — say the thing directly.
- Restating the same point three different ways to hit a word count. If it's said, move on.
- Clever-but-vague punchlines that sound smart but don't tell the reader what to do
  (e.g. never "the reps build the belief" — instead say exactly what to practice and how often).
- Inventing numbers, stats, client results, or claims the user did not actually tell you. If a
  chapter needs a proof point and the user hasn't given you one, use a plausible scenario framed
  as an example ("say you're trying to...") rather than a fabricated real result.
- Lists without narrative. A bullet list of tips with no story or reasoning behind it doesn't
  teach anything — always explain the why, not just the what.

## Every Moment an Unlock

Every chapter must deliver a non-obvious insight or new capability, not just information. Before
finalizing a chapter, check:
- Does the reader now know something they didn't before, stated plainly?
- Is there an exact action attached to it (not just an idea to "keep in mind")?
- Could this chapter be summarized as "here's some background on X"? If yes, it's not done —
  cut the background and get to the unlock.

## Matching a Voice Sample

If the user provides writing samples (a post, an email, a transcript):
- Match sentence length and rhythm, not just vocabulary.
- Match how directly they state opinions (hedged vs. blunt).
- Match their real phrases and language for their niche — don't overwrite with generic phrasing.
- Keep the guide in the USER's voice and brand only. Never import another creator's catchphrases,
  brand terms, or personal proof points into someone else's guide.

## Fallback Voice (no sample provided)

Use the Tone from `~/.claude/brand-profile.md` as a structural default only — swap in the user's
own niche, language, and story. If the profile is thin, default to:
- Direct, confident, no fluff.
- Opinion-first: lead with the point, then the explanation.
- Specifics and numbers over vague claims (using the user's real numbers, never invented ones).
- Never talks down to the reader; always treats them as capable.
