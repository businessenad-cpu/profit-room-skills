## 1. The one-sentence DNA

> Take one counterintuitive question about everyday life, answer it with three real-evidence "pillars," reframe a mundane modern experience as something profound we lost, and bookend the whole thing with the same concrete image.

## 2. The model script, annotated

### HOOK (0:00–0:30) — "mundane present → giant contrast → personal stakes → question"

Four moves, in order:

1. **Second-person, present-tense, mundane action:**
   > "Tonight, when the sun goes down, you're going to flip a switch. Light will flood the room and you won't think twice about it."
2. **The "But…" pivot + shock stat:**
   > "But for 99.9% of human history, that switch didn't exist."
3. **Vivid sensory deprivation + personal scale:**
   > "When the sun set, the world went dark. You couldn't even see your own hand in front of your face… For over 300,000 years, every single one of *your* ancestors spent roughly half their life in near total darkness."
4. **The title as an open question:**
   > "So what did they actually do for all those hours?"

Why it works: starts inside the viewer's own evening → yanks scale to 300,000 years → makes it personal ("your ancestors") → ends on an open loop the rest of the video closes.

### PROMISE (0:31–0:39) — the payoff stack

> "The answer changes everything we think we know about **sleep, storytelling, and what it means to be human.**"

Does double duty: raises stakes AND secretly lists the three body sections. **The three nouns in the promise = the three pillars.**

### PILLAR 1 — FIRE (0:39–2:54)
- **Transition in:** "Let's start with fire. Because fire is the reason any of this was possible."
- **Evidence anchor:** Wonderwerk Cave, South Africa, ~1 million years ago.
- **Mechanism:** the "circle of safety ~30 feet" — inside you live, outside you die. "Fire changed that equation overnight. Literally."
- **Mid-pillar spike:** "But here's what's fascinating." → fire added 2–4 usable hours a day.
- **The named study:** "In 2014, anthropologist Polly Wiesner published a study in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences…" → daytime = 80% practical talk; nighttime = **81% stories.**
- **Thesis line:** "The campfire was the original theater, the original classroom, the original church… It's where we became us."

### PILLAR 2 — TWO SLEEPS (2:54–5:45)
- **Transition in:** "But the night wasn't just about stories around the fire. There was something else happening — something scientists only rediscovered in the last 30 years."
- **Provocation:** "You were not designed to sleep the way you sleep right now."
- **Study #1 (discovery):** "In 2001, historian Roger Ekirch published a paper that changed sleep science…" → 500+ references → "first sleep" and "second sleep."
- **Reframe:** "This wasn't insomnia. This was normal."
- **Study #2 (confirmation):** "In 1992, psychiatrist Thomas Wehr at the National Institute of Mental Health ran an experiment…" → 14 hrs darkness → everyone fell into the two-sleep pattern → elevated **prolactin** (same as meditation/orgasm).
- **Thesis line:** "It wasn't just a gap in sleep — it was its own distinct state of consciousness."

Note the two-study move: one historian *discovers* the pattern in old records, one scientist *reproduces* it in a lab. Discovery + confirmation is more convincing than either alone.

### PILLAR 3 — HOW WE LOST IT (5:45–7:38)
- **Transition in (question):** "So what killed it? The answer is simpler than you'd think. Light."
- **Chronology of decline (dated milestones):** street lamps (1600s, Louis XIV) → cheap candles → gas lamps (1800s) → **Edison, 1879.**
- **Etymology aside (texture):** "the word curfew comes from the French *couvre-feu* — it literally means 'cover fire.'"
- **The cost today:** artificial/blue light → suppresses melatonin → fragments sleep; the one-week camping study that resets melatonin ~2 hours.
- **Thesis line:** "We didn't just change *when* we sleep. We eliminated an entire phase of human consciousness."

### RE-HOOK (7:47–7:58) — rhetorical question stack
> "Instead, we have insomnia. And we treat it like a disorder. But what if it's not a disorder at all? What if waking up at 2 a.m. and lying there in the dark isn't your body *malfunctioning*? What if it's your body *remembering what it was built to do?*"

Converts an abstract history lesson into a personal "oh, that's *me*" moment. This is the emotional peak.

### OUTRO (7:58–8:32) — poetic recap + bookend gut-punch
- Callback to the opening number ("For 300,000 years, your ancestors…") + a 3-beat poetic recap of the pillars (fires & stories → two-phase sleep → darkness so complete the Milky Way "was just the sky").
- **Final line returns to the exact opening image:**
  > "We traded all of that for a light switch. And most of us never even knew it was gone."

The light switch opens the video and closes it. Bookend shut.

## 3. The pillar skeleton (the repeating engine)

Every pillar runs the same six beats:

```
TRANSITION IN → PROVOCATION → REAL NAMED STUDY (name + year + institution + hard stat)
→ MECHANISM (how/why it worked) → REFRAME ("This wasn't X. This was Y.") → THESIS LINE
```

Vary the surface, keep the skeleton. Pillar 2 doubles the study beat (discovery + confirmation). Pillar 3 frequently becomes the "how we lost it / what it costs" turn and ends the body on a loss.

## 4. Transition phrase bank (adapt, don't copy verbatim every time)

| Function | Phrase |
|---|---|
| Open the first pillar | "Let's start with **X**. Because X is the reason any of this was possible." |
| Mid-pillar curiosity spike | "But here's what's fascinating." / "And here's the part nobody talks about." |
| Bridge to next pillar | "But it wasn't just about **X**. There was something else happening…" |
| Set up a reveal | "…something scientists only rediscovered in the last [N] years." |
| Reframe a fact | "This wasn't **[ordinary thing]**. This was **[profound thing]**." |
| Introduce evidence | "In [YEAR], [TITLE] [NAME] at [INSTITUTION] published a study / ran an experiment…" |
| Pose the turn | "So what killed it? The answer is simpler than you'd think." |
| Land the philosophical hit | "But what if it's not **X** at all? What if it's actually **Y**?" |
| Bookend close | "We traded all of that for **[bookend object]**. And most of us never even knew it was gone." |

## 5. The fill-in template

**[HOOK — 4 beats, ~70 words]** — shape is fixed (mundane → contrast → stakes → question); the *contrast* depends on topic type:
1. "Tonight/Today, you're going to **[mundane action the viewer does]**, and you won't think twice about it."
2. The contrast pivot — pick the one that's actually true:
   - *Lost-over-history:* "But for **[huge % / time span]**, **[that thing] didn't exist.**"
   - *Hidden-in-plain-sight:* "But stop and look at what's actually happening. **[Shocking hidden-scale or strangeness stat about a thing they do constantly — e.g. 'you'll spend six years of your life doing this and then delete every trace of it'].**"
3. "**[Vivid concrete detail that drives the scale home].** For over **[N] years** / every single **[night/day]**, **[the strange or opposite reality].**"
4. "So **[the title, asked as an open question]**?"

> The model video is a *lost-over-history* hook (the light switch). For a *hidden-in-plain-sight* topic (dreams, why we cry, what your gut does), do NOT force a "for most of history this didn't exist" line — it will be false. Keep the four beats; swap the contrast for hidden scale or strangeness.

**[PROMISE — 1 line]**
"The answer changes everything we think we know about **[noun 1], [noun 2], and [noun 3].**" *(these three become the pillars)*

**[PILLAR 1 — noun 1]** transition → provocation → real study (name+year+institution+stat) → mechanism → reframe → thesis ("this is where [big idea] was born").

**[PILLAR 2 — noun 2]** "But it wasn't just about [pillar 1]…" → repeat the skeleton with two studies (discovery + confirmation).

**[PILLAR 3 — noun 3 / the heaviest payoff]** *Lost-over-history:* "So what killed it? [one word]." → dated chronology of decline → one etymology/origin aside → the cost today → thesis ("We didn't just change X. We eliminated Y"). *Hidden-in-plain-sight:* the deepest mechanism or highest-stakes reveal → thesis ("It was never X. It was Y."). Either way it's the most profound pillar — end the body here.

**[RE-HOOK]** "We call it [modern label] and treat it like a problem. But what if it's not a problem — what if it's [the thing we lost]?"

**[OUTRO]** callback the opening number → 3-beat poetic recap of the pillars → final line on the **exact opening image** + "and most of us never even knew."

## 6. Style rules (non-negotiables)

1. **Second person throughout** — "you," "your ancestors," "your body." Never abstract. This is the #1 thing that separates this format from a Wikipedia voiceover.
2. **Every claim gets a name, a year, and an institution — and it must be real.** Specificity is the authority. Fabricating a study is the one unforgivable error (see SKILL.md anti-fabrication rule).
3. **Hard numbers as hooks:** 99.9%, 300,000 years, 81%, 30 feet, 14 hours, 500 references. Front-load them.
4. **The reframe sentence is the engine:** short, two beats. "This wasn't insomnia. This was normal."
5. **Bookend with one concrete object.** Open on it, close on it.
6. **One counterintuitive idea per video.** Everything serves the single question in the title.
7. **Sentences are short and spoken, not written.** Lots of fragments. Built for a narrator over B-roll, not for the page.

## 7. Common failure modes (and the fix)

- **Generic / encyclopedic.** Cause: no real specifics, or third-person drift. Fix: add real named studies; rewrite every "people" as "you."
- **Fabricated authority.** A study that sounds real but isn't. Fix: research first; only cite what you verified; phrase un-verifiable points without a fake number.
- **Pillars that don't escalate.** Three flat facts. Fix: order them discovery → deeper mechanism → cost/loss, so the stakes climb. End the body on what we lost.
- **No emotional turn.** All information, no revelation. Fix: the re-hook must recast something the viewer personally lives with.
- **Open and close don't rhyme.** Fix: choose the bookend object before drafting and aim the final line at it.
- **Reads like an article.** Fix: read it aloud; cut anything that doesn't sound like a person talking over footage.
