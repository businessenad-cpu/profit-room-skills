# LinkedIn Post Formats

Four validated formats. Choose based on the post angle — not randomly.
The hook, CTA, and PS line are always present regardless of format.

Anywhere you see `{{PROOF}}`, `{{OFFER}}`, `{{PRICE}}`, `{{LINK}}`, or `{{TRANSFORMATION}}`, pull the value from the brand profile at `~/.claude/brand-profile.md` (Proof/wins, Offer, price, Booking/checkout link, Transformation). Never invent numbers.

---

## Format A — Lead Magnet / Transformation

**Use when:** Offering a downloadable resource, guide, or swipe file. Or when the hook is "I went from X to zero." Historically the highest comment ceiling.

```
[HOOK — "I went from [time-based pain] to zero." OR "[Tool] grew my [platform] from [N] to [N]+."]

[Bridge — "Here's how:" or "Here's what's inside:"]

[Named tool or credibility anchor — 1 line]

→ [What it does — specific, tool named]
→ [What it eliminates — pain removed]
→ [Time or cost saved — number]
→ No [expensive thing]. No [time thing]. Just [simple thing].
→ [Platform or scale signal]
→ [Built/runs in short time]

[Pain reframe — "The biggest constraint isn't [wrong thing]. It's [right thing]."]

[Proof anchor — a line built from {{PROOF}}. This is the system.]

Comment "[KEYWORD]" and I'll send you the link.

PS - [Cost-of-inaction or identity stakes — 1-2 sentences. See PS Rules below.]
```

**Rules:**
- Embed at least one `No [expensive thing]. No [time thing]. Just [simple thing].` line inside the arrow list.
- Add a cost anchor line only if the user's profile documents one and the post involves tooling costs.
- Proof anchor line: vary the framing, keep the numbers accurate to {{PROOF}}.

## Format B — Personal Story / Moment

**Use when:** The post is a narrative — a mistake, a realization, a before/after that's story-driven rather than system-driven.

**Source material:** Use a real Before → Shift → After arc from the user's own experience or a client's (from the profile's Proof/wins). The Before is the reader-identification hook, the Shift is often the strongest line, the After is the payoff. Lead with the Before — let the reader feel seen before offering anything.

**Note:** Personal story posts should still carry a keyword CTA. Story posts without one tend to get resonance without conversion.

```
[HOOK — bold claim or confession: "40+ videos. Zero shorts."]

[Setup — what happened, one line of context]

[Story — 3-4 short paragraphs, each one idea]
[Problem → tension → shift → result]

[Lesson — 1-2 lines, the transferable insight]

[Proof anchor — optional but recommended, from {{PROOF}}]

Comment "[KEYWORD]" and I'll send you the link.

PS - [Cost-of-inaction or identity stakes — 1-2 sentences. See PS Rules below.]
```

## Format C — Curator / Authority

**Use when:** There's a large volume of something (tools, templates, tactics) that is mostly noise and the user is the filter.

```
[HOOK — "[Big number] [things] exist. Most are garbage."]

[Bridge — "Here's what actually works:"]

→ [Curated item 1 — why it's not garbage]
→ [Curated item 2]
→ [Curated item 3]
(5-7 items)

Comment "[KEYWORD]" and I'll send you the link.

PS - [Cost-of-inaction or identity stakes — 1-2 sentences. See PS Rules below.]
```

---

## Format D — Third-Party / Client Proof

**Use when:** A client, member, or someone the user helped has a result worth sharing. Underused and high-performing — use it more.

**Source material:** Use a real Before → Shift → After arc from the profile's Proof/wins. The Before becomes the hook, the Shift becomes the proof moment, the After becomes the payoff.

**Naming rule:** Never name a person without explicit consent. Default to "a [person type] I worked with" + their specific numbers and outcome. The specifics (dollars, timeframe, follower count) make it credible — not the name. Only name someone if the user confirms they have consent on file.

```
[HOOK — "A [person type] I worked with [specific outcome] with [tool/timeframe]."]

[What they built or did — 2-3 lines, specific]

[What made it possible — the system or tool]

[Bridge to audience: "You don't need [prerequisite they think they need]."]

Comment "[KEYWORD]" and I'll send you the link.

PS - [Cost-of-inaction or identity stakes — 1-2 sentences. See PS Rules below.]
```

## CTA Destination

Always points to the user's offer ({{OFFER}}) by default. Only change if the user explicitly specifies (an upsell, a lead magnet, or a pure value post).

**Name the offer in the post** — in the bridge line immediately before the CTA. Example: "That's what we build inside {{OFFER}}." or "2,000+ people are doing this inside {{OFFER}}."

**The CTA line itself is always:** `Comment "KEYWORD" and I'll send you the link.` — clean, no duplication of the offer name. The line above provides the context. Never append the offer name to the CTA line.

Default DM message (wired via your comment-to-DM tool, from the brand profile):
> "Hey {{firstName}}! Here's the link: {{LINK}} — {{PRICE}}. Inside you'll find {{OFFER}} — {{TRANSFORMATION}}. See you inside."

## PS Rules

Every post ends with a PS instead of a repost line. The PS is the last thing people read — even when they skim.

**The move:** Make inaction feel more expensive than acting. Never sell in the PS. Remove the last reason not to act.

**Choose one of these formulas — match to the post's core objection or audience resistance:**

- **Cost of inaction:** "If you're [doing the painful thing] every week, you're already paying the price. You're just not getting anything for it."
- **Identity split:** "[Person type A] will read this and keep doing what they're doing. [Person type B] will comment and have a system by Friday."
- **Reframe the decision:** "You'll [do the thing] again this week no matter what. The question is whether it [old result] or [new result]."
- **Permission removal:** "You don't need [scary prerequisite]. You need [the simple thing]. That's it."
- **Mirror moment / challenge:** "Go back to your last 10 posts. Find the one that performed best. Then ask yourself why."

**PS rules:**
- 1-2 sentences max
- Never pitch the offer directly — the CTA already did that
- Never use an em dash
- Match the PS to the specific audience fear or objection the post was written to address
- Rotate the formula — don't default to identity split every time
