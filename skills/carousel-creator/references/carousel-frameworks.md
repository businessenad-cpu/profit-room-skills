# Carousel Frameworks

Five reusable blueprints. Pick ONE per carousel. Each follows the **Narrative First** rule (see SKILL.md Step 4): one big idea per slide, each builds on the last, only the System slide may be a list. Adapt the wording to the topic and to your brand voice — the skeletons below are the shape, not a script.

Every framework ends on a CTA slide driven by the CTA mode (your offer, or a lead-magnet keyword).

---

## 1. Checklist / Audit
**When to use:** actionable lists — "X things you need", "the audit I run", "signs you're doing this wrong." Great when the value is a concrete set of moves the reader can apply today.

**Skeleton:**
1. **Cover** — hook naming the checklist + payoff ("The 5-point audit that found $4k of leaks in an hour").
2. **The stakes** — one concrete sentence on what it costs to skip this (money/time), not a list.
3. **The System slide** — the checklist itself, numbered 1…N. This is the ONE allowed list slide.
4-5. **Depth on the 1-2 items people get wrong** — one idea each, in prose, with a proof point or example.
6. **Payoff** — what changes for the reader once they run the checklist on their own work.
7. **CTA.**

---

## 2. Myth vs Fact
**When to use:** contrarian takes, correcting a widely-held belief, "everyone tells you X — here's what actually works."

**Skeleton:**
1. **Cover** — name the myth as the hook ("More followers = more money. Wrong.").
2. **The myth, stated fairly** — why it *sounds* right and who repeats it. One idea.
3. **Why it fails** — the mechanism: the hidden cost of believing it. Make them feel it.
4. **The fact / reframe** — the truer model, stated simply. Lead with proof if you have it.
5. **The System slide** — how to actually do it (numbered steps). The only list.
6. **Payoff** — what the reader gains by dropping the myth.
7. **CTA.**

---

## 3. Before / After (Reframe)
**When to use:** transformations, "generic vs specific", showing a shift from a bad state to a good one.

**Skeleton:**
1. **Cover** — hook naming the transformation ("From 3 hours a post to 60 seconds").
2. **Before** — paint the old, painful state concretely (their status quo).
3. **The turning point** — the single realization or tool that changed it. Intrigue + one surprise.
4. **After** — the new state, led with a hard result/number.
5. **The System slide** — the exact steps that got from before to after. The only list.
6. **Payoff / seed** — map the after-state onto the reader's own situation; make the gap visible.
7. **CTA.**

---

## 4. Step-by-step
**When to use:** how-to guides, processes, systems the reader can replicate.

**Skeleton:**
1. **Cover** — hook = outcome + mechanism ("The 4-step system that writes my posts for me").
2. **Who it's for + the promise** — one sentence on the result they'll get.
3. **The System slide** — the numbered steps, tight. The only list slide.
4-6. **One slide per hard/high-value step** — expand the steps that need nuance, one idea each, in prose. Tease upcoming steps by number ("Step 3 is where most people quit").
7. **Payoff** — what the finished system does for them.
8. **CTA.**

Keep step-detail slides to 2 short sentences if they carry an image.

---

## 5. Teardown
**When to use:** analyzing a tool, a repo, a competitor, or a case study — "here's what actually makes this work."

**Skeleton:**
1. **Cover** — hook naming the subject + the tension it creates ("This free tool does what I paid $50/mo for").
2. **What it is** — one-line what + a credibility stat (stars, users, revenue). Optional screenshot.
3. **The problem it kills** — the bottleneck people hit before it.
4. **How it works** — the key mechanism, 2 sentences. Optional screenshot.
5. **What actually changes** — outcome statements for the reader, not feature bullets.
6. **Who it's for / not for** — YES / NO framing.
7. **The shift** — paint the picture of the person who adopts it (forward-looking tension).
8. **CTA.**

For a single-repo teardown, this maps directly onto `references/github-mode.md`.

## Hook patterns (any framework)
- Transformation: "From [bad state] to [good state] with [mechanism]"
- Number: "[N] [things] that [outcome] (most people miss #1)"
- Contrarian: "[Common belief]. Here's why that's costing you."
- Proof-led: "[Tool/system] found [hard number] in [timeframe]"
- Insider: "[N] [tools/moves] people are using right now"

Name the result AND the mechanism. A hook that names only the topic ("my content system") has no payoff — the reader won't swipe.
